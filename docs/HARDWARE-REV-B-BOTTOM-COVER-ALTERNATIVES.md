# Rev B screwless underside-cover alternatives — research comparison

**26 October 2026. Research and design decision only; no CAD shapes changed, printed samples or physically proven closures.** The user prefers R5 rounded corners, 156mm width, physically rotated Waveshare LCD for USB-C on left, media caps 4mm above face. LCD support screws are **accepted**; bottom lid should require **no screws and ideally no tools**. The earlier lift-out underside lid with four M2.5 screws is overengineered for the intended use. A subsequent slide-in version uses a 133.2×58.3×2mm lid, 0.4mm nominal surrounding clearance, two captured side rails, rear insertion slot and thumb tab, **but lacks a positive retention latch** and may slide loose if the desk unit is carried.

## Options researched

| Mechanism | Closure/removal | Main benefits | Design or print downsides | Preliminary fit with existing case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Two passive hooks + one finger-release snap ('hook-and-click')** | Insert front two printed hooks under a shallow frame lip, press rear down to click a deflecting tab, push accessible underside rear tab and lift to remove | **No screws, magnets, or long rear feed slot**; purposeful hold while lifted; few parts; single-handed opening possible | Needs a PETG-compatible flexure, clear print-layer orientation, tested clip strain and fingertip notch; rear tab may still break if forced | **Preferred first CAD experiment**, using existing underside hatch with revised seating flange, replacing rear-slot rails |
| **B. Slide in rails + small lock detent** | Keep current rear-entry Y-direction slide, add one rounded interference bead and matched dimple or an accessible flexible release tab | Retains already modelled rail layout and side capture; cover can't fall if detent correctly tuned | Needs long rear-wall slot (potential weakness/visible edge), tight but sliding FDM tolerance, catch wears; release if friction too high | **Second choice** if user prefers the sliding feel |
| **C. Locating tabs + small magnets** | Hook shallow locating tongues at front and snap rear down onto magnetic pockets; lift using underside finger recess | Fast, quiet, clean, easy to reopen without plastic flex fatigue | Extra parts/adhesive/polarity; pockets consume structural wall thickness; verify actual pull-force and keep magnetic/metal pieces off active antenna/component space | **Third choice** for a premium, frequently serviced lid |
| **D. Perimeter snap-fit cover (two to four clips)** | Press flat lid upward to click multiple cantilever hooks; pinch/release tabs to remove | No purchased hardware or rear slot; flush underside | Multiple clips may bind, hard to remove with fingers, fatigue, brittle orientations; needs clearance for full 133mm length and case printing warpage | Workable, but more clipping complexity than A |
| **E. Rear hinge + click catch** | Door pivots about integral pin/printed hinge, latch closes at front | Cover cannot be misplaced; accessible for frequent wiring service | Hinge adds protrusion/seam, can foul desktop placement/feet and USB routing, hinge axle and case wall consume space | Lower priority |
| **F. Friction-fit / press-only lid** | Push panel into lip until friction grips | Extremely simple geometry | No deliberate retention: vibration, wear, printed tolerance or lifting could dislodge it; can be too tight to open | **Avoid as the only holding feature** |

## Recommended mechanical concept (OPTION A) — **not an approved CAD revision**

1. Use **two rigid lead-in tongues/hooks near the front (low-height side of underside)** to engage internal front return lip. These carry one edge and register the lid without bending.
2. Close by pressing the **rear edge of lid upward from desk underside**. One **wide accessible PETG flexure** (or replaceable clip) deflects and engages a small catch on the case. A **recessed fingertip press area** on the underside allows release; a slim finger notch helps the cover lift away. Flush or slight inset cover, avoiding unstable feet.
3. **Delete continuous rear-wall feed throat** from the earlier slider in the future *alternative variant*; retain uninterrupted rear wall whenever possible for stiffness and appearance. Preserve four screw-mounted LCD brackets, USB-C left, centre-aligned key row, volume encoder and 156mm envelope.
4. Treat lid perimeter clearance as **printer/material dependent**; trial nominal 0.25–0.5mm per side only as a parameter sweep, not final spec. A printed **small hook/clip test coupon** before full-size cover should quantify insertion force, release effort and number of safe cycles. Print orientation must prevent split layers at flexure root; add generous fillets and a chamfered capture ramp. Test with intended PLA/PETG/other FDM material rather than trusting theoretical B-Rep compatibility.
5. **Real geometry blockers remain:** the underside opening depth near 59.1mm versus 68.3mm full Waveshare module and 98×60 fixing centres. Screen may be impossible to introduce/fasten through this aperture even with tool-free lid. The rear lid choice must not be confused with resolving the **screen insertion and driver/screw access path**; this needs a separate swept insertion/installation study. Any finger-latch and bracket must also clear the LCD rear components, PCB, cables, Wi-Fi antenna, RGB flat flex and MX underside.

## Other viable first-prototype choices

- If the user wants the existing pure **sliding motion**, **B** with a small tactile detent is closer to the design already built; verify removal force and overtravel stop in print. A plain friction slide is not a positive latch.
- If a premium seamless cover is valued over minimum bill of materials, **C** with two front hooks and *two* magnet pairs at rear is the cleanest lift-off option. Magnet count/size are only trial suggestions: first measure available wall thickness and choose safe pockets and polarity. No magnet purchases/installation decided.
- If a replaceable/breakable latch is a concern, separate the flexible element from the expensive body: put it on the separately printed lid, or use a replaceable tiny PETG latch insert. Plan clip strength by print direction and local wall geometry.

## Research references and applicability

- [Formlabs, snap-fit enclosure design](https://formlabs.com/eu/blog/designing-3d-printed-snap-fit-enclosures/): cantilever catch, lugs, flexure design and clearance caveats; largely SLA/SLS examples. **Use as a structural pattern, not authoritative FDM tolerance**.
- [Protolabs/Xometry UK MJF guide](https://xometry.pro/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/EN-UK-MJF-Design-Guide.pdf): draft/taper/fillets for flexures; **MJF parameters cannot be applied verbatim to desktop FDM**.
- [Qidi FDM interlocking clearance discussion](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/news/how-to-3d-print-interlocking-parts-and-assemblies): sliding rail trial gaps around 0.2–0.3mm per side and snap perimeters 0.2–0.25mm. These are **process examples**, not guaranteed fit for our printer/filament or 133mm span.
- [Parametric OpenSCAD enclosure closure styles](https://github.com/conyx/boxee.scad): lip, snap latch, magnets, hinge and optional slide locks; research reference only; no copying or third-party geometry imported.
- [Formlabs extended design PDF](https://formlabs-media.formlabs.com/rs/060-UIG-504/images/WP-EN-How-to-Design-and-3D-Print-Snap-Fit-Enclosures.pdf): sample cantilever engagement and catch details are example dimensions, not required for our case.
- [MakerWorld parametric sliding magnetic box](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1576534-customizable-parametric-sliding-magnetic-box): real maker-style model demonstrating magnetic slide retention (not proven by us).
- [Additive Inn build case study](https://www.additiveinn.com/projects/slider-modular-response-box): explicit trial-and-reprint iterations for magnetic/sliding electronics case demonstrating why real lid retention cannot be certified by CAD alone.
